Fiduciary Computation

Explicit causal boundaries for consequential systems.

A doctoral research programme investigating how information may acquire protected consequences only through explicit, bounded and independently inspectable computational boundaries.

Core concept

Modern systems are highly capable of producing information, recommendations and candidate actions. Fiduciary Computation studies a narrower question: under what explicitly declared conditions may a proposal acquire consequential authority?

Information≠Proposal≠Consequential Admission≠Consequential Authority≠Protected Execution≠Outcome

The research does not assume that information is true merely because it is recorded, signed, generated by AI, or accepted by software.

Consequential Admission

The explicit computational boundary at which a proposal is evaluated under declared conditions before it may acquire consequential authority.

Explicit Causal Boundary

A boundary through which consequence-changing dependencies must pass before they may legitimately influence a protected consequence.

Bounded Trust

Trust dependencies are declared and bounded relative to a specific protected consequence rather than treated as an undifferentiated property of an entire system.

Durable Fiduciary Deed · DFD

A research term for a durable representation of a constituted consequential act whose identity is distinguished from any particular carrier.

Consequential Authority

Bounded authority that exists after admission and before protected execution. Admission and execution remain distinct stages.

Protected Consequence

An effect whose execution is intentionally placed behind an explicit consequential boundary.

Research status

Pre-publication doctoral research. Formal semantics, proofs, algorithms, implementations, experimental harnesses, campaign data, benchmark results and unpublished manuscripts remain private pending appropriate academic dissemination.

What this page does not claim

FC is not presented here as a truth oracle, a legal-validity engine, a trustless system, a blockchain, or a completed security standard. This page records terminology and a research direction only.
